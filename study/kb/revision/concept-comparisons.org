* Model answers
** Five forces for an industry (e.g. regional transport operator)
1. Define the industry (regional passenger transport by bus).
2. Rivalry: few operators, high fixed costs, public tenders → medium-high.
3. Suppliers: bus makers and fuel are concentrated → medium.
4. Buyers: regional authority buys the service in tenders → high.
5. New entrants: tenders and fleet capital are barriers → low-medium.
6. Substitutes: car, train, car-sharing → high.
7. Conclusion: low attractiveness; strategy: cost leadership in tenders or
   differentiation on punctuality/digital service; support with value-chain
   activities (fleet maintenance, scheduling IT). Limits: static, single business.

** Volume–variety analysis
Place five settings: shipyard (project, fixed position), tool shop (jobbing,
functional), bakery batches (batch, functional/cellular), car assembly (line),
refinery (continuous). Explain why the layout follows the process and what
happens off the diagonal (cost vs flexibility).

** Purchasing portfolio
Run ABC on spend, then place items in Kraljic: a class-C single-source spare
can be a bottleneck; a class-A commodity is leverage. Choose sourcing: dual for
leverage, partnership for strategic, buffers or redesign for bottleneck,
simplification for non-critical.

* Assessment
An effective response contains a definition, a mechanism, a relevant example,
a justified implication and a limitation. Review the weakest component before
repeating the answer from memory.
