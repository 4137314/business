#+title: 5S, production leveling and improvement routines
#+summary: 5S organizes the workplace (sort, set in order, shine, standardize, sustain); heijunka levels volume and mix; SMED shortens set-ups; gemba walks put managers where the work is.

* Definition
| Japanese | English       | Meaning                                   |
|----------+---------------+-------------------------------------------|
| Seiri    | Sort          | remove what is not needed                 |
| Seiton   | Set in order  | a place for everything, everything in its |
|          |               | place                                     |
| Seiso    | Sweep / shine | clean and inspect                         |
| Seiketsu | Standardize   | make the first three routine              |
| Shitsuke | Sustain       | self-discipline, keep it up               |
Purpose: organize the workplace to optimize performance, cleanliness and
safety. Black & Decker's Hampstead (Maryland) plant: before/after — "one place
for each thing, each thing in its place"; simple actions raise productivity and
cut waste.

* Model
** Implementing 5S
1. Plan the action (materials, coordination with departments, schedule).
2. Form the team (essential).
3. Assess the work area, the *gemba*: map and photograph, define boundaries,
   run 5S assessments with a checklist.
4. Launch 5S: remove unnecessary items (red tag, "use or lose"), simplify
   access, clean.
5. Measure results and sustain.
Checklist questions — Sort: do all team members know the programme? are
criteria for necessary/unnecessary defined? have unnecessary items been
removed? do employees know the disposal procedure? Set in order: is there a
visually marked place for everything? is everything in place? Sweep: are work
areas, offices and rooms clean and orderly?
*Gemba walk*: go physically to the workplace, observe processes, talk with
those who work, identify real waste and problems.

** Heijunka (production leveling)
Distribute *volumes and product mix* evenly over time to make the flow regular
and stable: avoid peaks and troughs, reduce waste (stock, waiting, overload),
improve continuous flow. Part of JIT with mixed-model production, small lots and
*SMED* (single-minute exchange of die: set-up reduction).

* Worked example
Weekly demand: 200 A, 100 B, 100 C (5 days). Batch plan: Mon–Tue A, Wed B,
Thu C… → big stocks of A by Wednesday. Level plan: each day 40 A, 20 B, 20 C,
in a repeating sequence A-A-B-A-A-C → stable load and stock of one day only.
Requires short set-ups (SMED).

* Pitfall
5S is not a cleaning campaign: without standardize and sustain it decays.
Leveling smooths production, not customer demand itself.
