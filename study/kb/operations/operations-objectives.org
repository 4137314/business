#+title: Operations, the four Vs and performance objectives
#+summary: Operations management designs, plans, controls and improves the processes that transform inputs into goods and services; operations differ by volume, variety, variation and visibility, and are judged on quality, speed, dependability, flexibility, cost and sustainability.

* Definition
*Operations management*: managing the activities and processes through which an
organization transforms inputs (resources) into outputs (goods and services),
creating value for the customer — design, planning, control and improvement.
Transformed resources: materials, information, customers; transforming
resources: staff, plant and facilities. Three levels of analysis: *supply
network* (flow between operations), *operation* (flow between processes),
*process* (flow between resources). Operations and process management is the
largest consulting area (31% of revenue of the 40 largest firms).

*Products vs services*: a continuum. *Servitization*: a manufacturer adds a
growing service offer, moving from selling goods to selling product–service
solutions. *Tangible cues*: physical elements that let customers judge an
intangible service.

* Model
** The four Vs
| V          | High end: high unit cost          | Low end: low unit cost         |
|------------+-----------------------------------+--------------------------------|
| Volume     | (low volume) low repeatability,   | (high volume) repeatable,      |
|            | staff do many tasks, little       | specialized, systematized,     |
|            | systematization                   | capital intensive              |
| Variety    | (high) flexible, complex, matches | (low) well defined, routine,   |
|            | specific needs                    | standardized                   |
| Variation  | (high) adjustable capacity,       | (low) stable, predictable,     |
|            | anticipates demand                | high utilization               |
| Visibility | (high) low wait tolerance, judged | (low) time lag production–use, |
|            | on perception, customer skills    | standardized, lower skills     |
Low cost lives at: high volume, low variety, low variation, low visibility.
Example: a craft bicycle workshop (low volume, high variety) vs a mass bicycle
plant; in banking, the branch (high visibility) vs the back-office account
centre (low visibility). Clarks shoes: own plants, dedicated and non-dedicated
contractors, own and non-own sales network to absorb demand variation.

** Managing variety efficiently
Ford Model T: "any colour so long as it is black". Today: *mass customization*
— a standardized system allowing controlled variation (the customer sees
freedom, the firm sees modules), via modularity, product configurators,
3D printing, *assemble to order* and *postponement*.

** Performance objectives
| Objective      | Process meaning                        |
|----------------+----------------------------------------|
| Quality        | error-free, adequate resources         |
| Speed          | meet demand quickly                    |
| Dependability  | reliable timing and volumes            |
| Flexibility    | resources with a range of capabilities |
| Cost           | adequate capacity, no waste            |
| Sustainability | energy, ecological footprint           |
Micro-performance variables of a process: arrival rate, throughput rate,
throughput time, number of units in process (WIP), utilization of resources;
driven by variability of arrivals, configuration, capacity, variability of
activities. Little's law links them:
\( \text{WIP} = \text{throughput rate} \times \text{throughput time} \).
*Sandcone model*: improvement builds cumulatively — quality first, then
dependability, speed, flexibility, and finally cost rests on all of them.

* Worked example
A help desk handles 20 tickets/hour with 60 tickets in the system on average:
throughput time \( = 60 / 20 = 3 \) hours. To promise 1.5 hours, halve WIP or
double the processing rate.

* Pitfall
High variety is not bad: it is costly and must be priced or managed (modules).
Visibility concerns the customer's exposure to the process, not marketing.
