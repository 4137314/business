#+title: Fisher: efficient and responsive supply chains
#+summary: Fisher (1997) matches functional products (predictable demand) with physically efficient (lean) chains and innovative products (unpredictable demand) with market-responsive (agile) chains; mismatches destroy value.

* Definition
Products differ:
| Feature             | Functional | Innovative |
|---------------------+------------+------------|
| Cost of a lost sale | low        | high       |
| Obsolescence risk   | low        | high       |
| Forecast accuracy   | high       | low        |
| Product variety     | low        | high       |
| Life cycle          | > 2 years  | < 1 year   |
| Gross margin        | < 35%      | > 35%      |
Think of a product by life cycle, margin and the [[id:operations-objectives][four Vs]]. Example: Apple's
suppliers expected iPhone 6s orders cut by 30% in one quarter — innovative
products have volatile demand.

* Model
| Aspect          | Efficient (lean)             | Responsive (agile)               |
|-----------------+------------------------------+----------------------------------|
| Supply goal     | efficiency: meet predictable | respond fast to unpredictable    |
|                 | demand at lowest cost        | demand; minimize stock-outs,     |
|                 |                              | markdowns, obsolete stock        |
| Inventory       | high turns, minimize stock   | significant buffers of parts and |
|                 |                              | finished goods; postponement     |
| Lead time       | reduce if it adds no cost    | reduce aggressively              |
| Supplier choice | cost                         | speed, flexibility               |
| Product design  | integral, max performance at | modular, to enable postponement  |
|                 | min cost                     |                                  |
| Product \ chain | Efficient                    | Responsive                       |
|-----------------+------------------------------+----------------------------------|
| Functional      | match                        | mismatch                         |
| Innovative      | mismatch                     | match                            |

*Lean supply chain*: JIT; basic contracts with relational elements; sourcing on
total cost, not price; local sourcing; fewer suppliers; shortest possible chain;
pull. Challenges: JIT needs frequent deliveries — hard with global low-cost
suppliers; green issues; more exposure to disruption.
*Agile supply chain*: "responds quickly to sudden changes in demand or supply,
handles external disruptions smoothly and cost-efficiently" (Lee 2007); speed =
lead time, flexibility = range and response. Paths: supply side (spatial
integration, partner capabilities, reliable logistics), operations (cut
non-value time, anticipate demand, buffers, contingency plans, flexible
production), demand side (information integration, product architecture,
postponement, market sensitivity).

* Worked example
Pasta (functional: stable demand, low margin) → efficient chain: full trucks,
long runs, low stock. A new smartphone (innovative: < 1 year life, high margin)
→ responsive chain: buffer capacity, fast air freight, modular design. A
fashion retailer serving basic T-shirts and trendy items can run both — basics
lean, trend items agile — combined via postponement, modularity and platforms.

* Pitfall
Responsive is not "inefficient": it spends on speed where lost sales cost more.
Functional products in a responsive chain waste money; innovative ones in an
efficient chain lose sales and pile up obsolete stock.
