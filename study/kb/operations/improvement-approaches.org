#+title: TQM, continuous improvement and BPR
#+summary: Breakthrough improvement (BPR) makes big, abrupt, technology-driven changes; continuous improvement (TQM, Lean, Six Sigma) makes small steps with everyone; each approach mixes emphasis on solutions vs methods and rapid vs gradual change.

* Model
| Aspect      | Breakthrough (BPR)             | Continuous (TQM, Six Sigma, Lean) |
|-------------+--------------------------------+-----------------------------------|
| Duration    | short term                     | long term                         |
| Pace        | big steps                      | small steps                       |
| Change      | abrupt                         | incremental                       |
| Involvement | a few "champions"              | everyone                          |
| Approach    | individualistic                | collective                        |
| Stimulus    | inventions, new technology     | conventional know-how             |
| Risks       | concentrated                   | spread over many projects         |
| Investment  | large investment, small effort | small investment, large effort    |
| Focus       | technology                     | people                            |
| Evaluation  | profit                         | better results in general         |
Positioning: BPR = rapid change, emphasis on *solutions* (what to do); Six Sigma
= solutions with more method; Lean = methods, fairly gradual; TQM = methods
(how to do it), gradual change.

** Business Process Reengineering
Discontinuous change: radically rethink processes rather than improve them bit
by bit — a break with the past. Appropriate when: (1) performance is very
unsatisfactory; (2) strong market change; (3) disruptive technologies (do not
"glue" technology on old processes); (4) mergers, acquisitions, major
reorganizations with duplicated processes; (5) crisis/turnaround; (6) obsolete,
over-complex processes (too many approvals, red tape). Elements: fundamental
change, end-to-end processes, customer centricity.

** Total Quality Management
Deming and Juran move quality from *downstream inspection* to a responsibility
of the whole organization. Elements: customer relationships, include all
people, improvement cycles (PDCA), systems and procedures, education.
*Jidoka*: "automation with a human touch" (autonomation).
| Aspect   | TQM                     | Lean                             |
|----------+-------------------------+----------------------------------|
| Goal     | total quality, zero     | eliminate waste (muda)           |
|          | defects                 |                                  |
| Focus    | product/service quality | process flow, customer value     |
| Approach | cultural, managerial    | operational, practical           |
| Origin   | USA (Deming, Juran)     | Japan (Toyota)                   |
| Tools    | PDCA, quality audits,   | value-stream mapping, Kanban, 5S |
|          | standards               |                                  |
| Problems | errors, defects         | waiting, waste, inefficiency     |

* Example
A bank merging two IT systems with duplicated account-opening processes → BPR
(redesign one process from zero). A factory with a stable process and 2% scrap
→ continuous improvement with teams (kaizen, Six Sigma projects).

* Pitfall
BPR is not "cutting staff"; continuous improvement is not "no investment".
They can be combined: a radical redesign followed by continuous improvement.
