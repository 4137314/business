#+title: Quality, service gaps and quality costs
#+summary: Quality can be defined in five ways (Garvin) plus the gap view, has several dimensions, is certified by ISO 9001, and has prevention, appraisal, internal and external failure costs whose optimum shifts towards prevention in the total-quality view.

* Definition
Diagnostic logic: is quality *defined*? *measured*? *controlled*? how are
*causes* identified? → *improvement* (iterative). "Which hamburger has the best
quality — McDonald's or a gourmet burger?" depends on the definition.
| Garvin (1984)       | Quality is...                     |
|---------------------+-----------------------------------|
| Product-based       | a set of specific characteristics |
| Manufacturing-based | absence of defects (conformance)  |
| Value-based         | quality relative to cost/price    |
| User-based          | fitness for purpose               |
| Transcendent        | innate, intangible excellence     |
| (+) Gap-based       | expectations vs perceptions       |
ANSI/ASQC: "the totality of features and characteristics of a product or
service that bear on its ability to satisfy given needs".

* Model
** Dimensions
Performance (main features), aesthetics (look, feel, smell, taste), features
(extras), conformance (to customer expectations/specs), reliability
(consistency), durability (useful life), perceived quality (reputation),
after-sales service (complaints, satisfaction). Tool: [[id:design-quality-tools][QFD]].

** ISO 9001
International standard for any organization; eight principles: customer focus,
leadership, involvement of people, process approach, system approach to
management, continual improvement, factual decision making, mutually
beneficial supplier relations.

** Gap view
Customer expectations vs perceived service: perceived quality is poor if
expectations exceed perceptions, good if perceptions exceed expectations
(Parasuraman, Zeithaml, Berry: [[id:positioning][SERVQUAL]] five gaps).

** Measures and standards
Objective or subjective? absolute or relative? who judges — customers, staff,
managers? Standards: historical, strategic (targets), external (competitors),
absolute. Different standards give different messages: actual 83% is *good* vs
last year's 60% and vs competitors' 75%, *poor* vs the 95% strategic goal and
vs absolute 100%.

** Costs of quality
| Category         | Includes                                        |
|------------------+-------------------------------------------------|
| Prevention       | planning quality, design improvement, training, |
|                  | process control                                 |
| Appraisal        | sampling plans, inspection and test, audits,    |
|                  | customer surveys                                |
| Internal failure | scrap, rework, lost time, troubleshooting       |
| External failure | lost goodwill, returns, litigation, warranty,   |
|                  | excess service capacity                         |
Traditional view: prevention+appraisal costs rise with effort, failure costs
fall → an *optimum* level of quality effort. Modern (TQM) view: prevention
reduces failures more than it costs, so total cost keeps falling — aim at
zero defects.

* Worked example
Before: prevention 10, appraisal 30, internal failure 60, external 100 → 200.
After training and poka-yoke: prevention 40, appraisal 20, internal 25,
external 30 → 115. Spending 30 more on prevention saved 115.

* Pitfall
Conformance is one of eight dimensions, not the whole of quality. Appraisal
finds defects; only prevention avoids them.

* Recall
List Garvin's five definitions and the gap view; eight dimensions; four cost
categories; compare traditional and TQM cost curves.
