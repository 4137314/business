#+title: The value chain and strategic groups
#+summary: Strategic groups map competitors on the variables that separate them; the value chain breaks a business unit into primary and support activities so that the margin (value minus cost) can be traced to each activity.

* Model
** Strategic groups (raggruppamenti strategici)
Strategic mapping represents graphically the competitors of an industry in
homogeneous groups, based on aggregation variables. Possible dimensions:
production specialization, degree of vertical integration, cost structure and
level, product quality, brand identification, distribution channels, range
breadth, geographic extension. Choose variables expressing *mobility barriers*
(brand, sales networks) and reflecting different strategic actions; often draw
several maps.

US airline case: nine main firms (American, United, Delta, America West,
Southwest, TWA, NWA, USAir, Continental); variables *cost per ASM* (available
seat-mile: easy to compute, similar cost structures; costs are inversely
proportional to average flight distance) and *quality* AQR (Airline Quality
Rating, National Institute for Aviation Research, Wichita State University).
| Group               | Members                      | Position                |
|---------------------+------------------------------+-------------------------|
| Differentiation     | American, United, Delta      | high quality            |
| Cost leadership     | America West                 | low cost, low quality   |
| Focus               | Southwest                    | quality niche, low cost |
| Stuck in the middle | TWA, NWA, USAir, Continental | low quality, high cost  |

** The value chain
Built for a single strategic business unit (SBU), interpreted inside the wider
*value system*: value chains of suppliers → the SBU → channels → customers.
\[ \text{Margin} = \text{Value created} - \text{Costs incurred} \]
Value = total revenue = price × units sold, i.e. what buyers are willing to pay.
| Primary activity    | Content                                           |
|---------------------+---------------------------------------------------|
| Inbound logistics   | receiving, storage, handling, warehouse, stock    |
|                     | control, vehicle scheduling, returns to suppliers |
| Operations          | machining, assembly, packaging, maintenance       |
| Outbound logistics  | finished-goods storage, delivery vehicles, order  |
|                     | scheduling and processing                         |
| Marketing and sales | advertising, sales force, offers, channel choice, |
|                     | pricing policy                                    |
| After-sales service | installation, testing, training, spare parts,     |
|                     | assistance                                        |

| Support activity       | Content                                          |
|------------------------+--------------------------------------------------|
| Procurement            | procedure for buying materials, supplies, assets |
| Technology development | know-how, procedures, technological input        |
| Human resources        | selection, hiring, evaluation, careers, unions   |
| Firm infrastructure    | general management, planning, finance,           |
|                        | accounting, legal, quality control               |
Primary activities create the product physically, transfer it to the buyer and
assist after sale — tied to the operating process. Support activities sustain
primary ones and also generate value. Analysing each activity's contribution is
a *disaggregation* process: e.g. marketing and sales split into advertising,
sales-force management, pricing, promotion, channels.

* Worked example
Coca-Cola's global differentiation is supported by: logistics and production →
economies of scale; marketing and sales → iconic brand; R&D (technology
development) → new products; procurement → purchasing power. IKEA's cost
leadership lives in outbound logistics (customer self-transport) and operations
(flat-pack design).

* Pitfall
The value chain belongs to one business unit, not to a whole group. Support
activities are not "overhead without value". Margin is value minus *all*
activity costs.

draw the airline strategic map and name its four groups.
