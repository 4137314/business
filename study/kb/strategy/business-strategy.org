#+title: Strategy, objectives and competitive advantage
#+summary: Strategy links external opportunities with internal resources; it answers "where to compete" (corporate) and "how to compete" (business), and evolved from the S-C-P paradigm to Porter's Value School and the resource-based view.

* Definition
Strategy is, in extreme synthesis, the *link between the opportunities the firm
perceives in the external environment and the resources it has or intends to
acquire* to seize them. It requires *strategic fit* among three elements:
| Element              | Content                                            |
|----------------------+----------------------------------------------------|
| External environment | competitors, customers, suppliers                  |
| Firm                 | objectives and values, resources and capabilities, |
|                      | structure and systems                              |
| Strategy             | the fit between the two                            |
The main objective of strategy is to ensure that management yields a *return on
capital higher than its cost*.

"Strategy without tactics is the slowest route to victory; tactics without
strategy is the noise before defeat" (Sun Tzu).
| Strategy                                   | Tactics                           |
|--------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------|
| general medium-to-long-term plan of action | specific action inside a strategy |
| aimed at "winning the war"                 | aimed at "winning the battles"    |
| hard to reverse in the short run           | easier to reverse                 |
/Strategy/ is the *content* of fundamental choices on the firm–environment
relation; /strategic planning/ is their *form*: the decision, operating and
time procedure that matches strategy objectives with available resources.

* Model
** Two strategic questions, three levels
| Question / level    | Content                                                    |
|---------------------+------------------------------------------------------------|
| Where to compete?   | *corporate (group) strategy*: industries and markets with  |
|                     | above-average profit; allocates financial and managerial   |
|                     | resources among businesses                                 |
| How to compete?     | *business (competitive) strategy*: the way of competing in |
|                     | one business to earn above-average returns                 |
| Functional strategy | implements business strategy in production, marketing,     |
|                     | finance, HR                                                |
*Strategic scope* (/ambito strategico/): the horizon of competition — which
markets, which products for each market, which geographic areas, which
technologies.

** Two determinants of competitive strategy
- *Industry attractiveness* — depends on competitive factors (concentration,
  economies of scale, entry and exit barriers, product differentiation,
  information) which shape prices, costs and investments, hence the profitability
  of the whole industry.
- *Competitive position* — where the firm stands inside its industry determines
  whether its profitability is above or below the industry average.
Both are dynamic: strategy can change them.

** An industry (settore)
Set of firms producing goods or services in direct competition, perceived by
demand as substitutes: complete substitutability (identical products) or
partial (product differentiation). Market forms: perfect competition,
oligopoly, monopoly.

** Evolution of strategic thought
| School                   | Claim                                                 |
|--------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------|
| S-C-P paradigm (Harvard) | Structure → Conduct → Performance: industry structure |
|                          | drives behaviour and profitability (strong version:   |
|                          | strategies do not matter)                             |
| Value School (Porter)    | two-way influence: strategy can modify the industry   |
|                          | structure; performance is not only structural         |
| Resource-based view      | advantage comes from internal resources and           |
|                          | competences ([[id:resource-based-view][RBV]])         |
| Integrated approach      | Porter (market, external) + RBV (resources, internal) |
Structural variables in S-C-P: *concentration* (market power over demand and
supply), *economies of scale* (average cost falls as capacity grows), *barriers
to entry/exit*, *product differentiation* (goods meet the same need but buyers
recognize differences), *information* (unevenly distributed among sellers and
buyers).

The Value School's tools: external environment → [[id:five-forces][five forces]];
competition → [[id:value-chain][strategic groups]]; internal environment →
[[id:value-chain][value chain]].

* Example
A car maker decides to enter the e-bike industry (corporate: where); it chooses
to compete through premium design and dealer service (business: how); marketing
then sets channels and prices (functional). A price cut for one quarter is a
tactic, not a strategy.

* Pitfall
Corporate strategy chooses businesses; business strategy chooses the way to win
in one of them. S-C-P is deterministic; Porter adds that the firm can act on the
structure.

* Recall
Define strategy and strategic fit; give the two questions and three levels;
contrast strategy and tactics; summarize S-C-P → Porter → RBV.
