#+title: Customer value and the marketing process
#+summary: Marketing creates value for customers and builds profitable relationships to capture value in return; it starts from needs, wants and demand and splits into strategic (analysis) and operational (action) marketing.

* Definition
Marketing is the process by which firms *create value for customers* and build
strong relationships with them *in order to capture value in return*.
| Concept | Meaning                                | Pizza example    |
|---------+----------------------------------------+------------------|
| Need    | state of felt deprivation              | hunger           |
| Want    | need shaped by culture and personality | wanting a pizza  |
| Demand  | want backed by purchasing power        | buying the pizza |
Needs are intrinsic to human nature and of three types — *functional* (an
object/service: transport), *psychological* (an attitude/value: moving safely),
*social* (a sign/symbol: being noticed while moving). Perception of a need is
subjective; one product can meet several needs, one need can be met by several
products.

*Market offering*: combination of products, services, information or
experiences offered to satisfy a need. *Marketing myopia*: paying more
attention to the products than to the benefits and experiences they produce.
*Exchange*: obtaining a desired good from someone by offering something in
return — keep and grow existing customers, not only attract new ones. *Market*:
set of actual and potential buyers.

* Model
** Value and satisfaction: setting expectations
Marketers must set the *right level of expectations*: neither too high
(disappointment) nor too low (no attraction). "What you order vs what you receive".
| Expectations \ experience | Poor experience | Great experience |
|---------------------------+-----------------+------------------|
| High expectations         | disappointed    | devoted          |
| Low expectations          | disaffected     | delighted        |
Perceived quality is poor when expectations > perceptions, good when
expectations < perceptions ([[id:quality-management][gap model]]).

** Strategic planning hierarchy
Mission → objectives → business portfolio → functional/product/market strategy
→ marketing strategy. The *mission* should be market-oriented (products and
technologies become obsolete, customer needs remain). Objectives are cascaded
to every manager; e.g. strategic objective "invest in advertising, strategic
partnerships", marketing strategy "improve the perceived quality of McDonald's
Italy". Portfolio: see [[id:portfolio-and-growth][BCG and Ansoff]].

** Customer-driven marketing strategy
Two key questions: *who are our customers?* (segmentation and targeting) and
*how can we serve them best?* (differentiation and positioning, the *value
proposition* — the set of benefits and values promised; Value Proposition Canvas).

** Five marketing orientations
| Concept            | Focus                                | Example      |
|--------------------+--------------------------------------+--------------|
| Production         | availability and low cost            | H&M          |
| Product            | quality, performance, features       | a laptop     |
| Selling            | large-scale selling and promotion    | Allianz      |
| Marketing          | customer needs, integrated marketing | Apple iPhone |
| Societal marketing | consumer wants + firm + long-term    | sustainable  |
|                    | interests of consumers and society   | brands       |
Selling concept: factory → existing products → selling and promoting → profit
through *sales volume*. Marketing concept: market → customer needs → integrated
marketing → profit through *customer satisfaction*.

** Capturing value
- *Marketing mix*: tools implementing the strategy (4P, [[id:marketing-mix][marketing mix]]);
  integrated marketing programme combines them.
- *CRM*: creating, maintaining and growing profitable relationships by offering
  superior value and satisfaction.
- *Customer lifetime value*: value of the whole stream of purchases of a
  customer loyal for life. *Share of customer*: share of the customer's
  purchases in the category won by the firm. *Customer equity*: total lifetime
  value of all the firm's customers.
- Customer journey: awareness → consideration → conversion → loyalty → advocacy.

** Strategic vs operational marketing (Lambin)
| Strategic marketing                | Operational marketing                   |
|------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------|
| analysis-oriented                  | action-oriented                         |
| needs analysis, segmentation,      | product, distribution, price,           |
| attractiveness and competitive     | promotion → marketing programme         |
| analysis → positioning strategy    |                                         |
| which market, segment, positioning | reach a market share with the 4P levers |
| medium to long term                | short to medium term                    |

* Worked example
Customer spends EUR 40 a week in supermarkets, 50% with our chain (share of
customer), for 20 years: lifetime revenue \( 40 \times 52 \times 20 \times 0.5
= \text{EUR } 20{,}800 \). Raising the share to 60% adds EUR 4,160 without
acquiring a single new customer.

* Pitfall
A need is not created by marketing; wants and demand are shaped. Selling ≠
marketing: selling starts from the factory, marketing from the market.

* Recall
Define marketing; explain need/want/demand with the pizza; draw the
expectations × experience matrix; list five orientations with examples;
contrast strategic and operational marketing.
