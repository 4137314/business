#+title: Liquidity, working capital and treasury margin
#+summary: The financial reclassification sorts uses (assets) and sources (liabilities and equity) by 12-month liquidity or maturity; treasury margin, net working capital and the acid-test ratio measure the ability to meet short-term obligations.

* Model
** Analysis process
Acquire two or more statements (the firm's, competitors', industry) → check
reliability and formal review → choose a reclassification model → re-elaborate
data → choose analysis technique → evaluate: should we buy from this firm?
grant it credit? how does it perform against competitors? Analysis informs on
asset structure, profitability of investments, solvency and liquidity,
structural efficiency, ability to generate cash.

** Financial reclassification of the balance sheet
| Uses (impieghi)                       | Sources (fonti)                         |
|---------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------|
| Current assets AC (within 12 months): | Current liabilities Pcor: payables,     |
| immediate liquidity Li, deferred      | provisions, TFR, accruals due within 12 |
| liquidity Ld (short receivables,      | months                                  |
| accruals), inventories R              |                                         |
| Fixed assets AI: net fixed assets and | Long-term liabilities Pcon: same items  |
| items realizable after 12 months      | due after 12 months                     |
|                                       | Equity PN (own capital)                 |
\[ MT = L_i + L_d - P_{cor} \qquad CCN = AC - P_{cor} \qquad
   IL = \frac{AC - R}{P_{cor}} = \frac{L_i + L_d}{P_{cor}} \]
- *Treasury margin MT*: can liquid and soon-liquid assets pay short-term
  debts? should be ≥ 0.
- *Net working capital CCN*: can current assets (stock included) cover
  short-term debts? A negative CCN is worse than a negative MT: not even selling
  inventory would satisfy short-term creditors.
- *Liquidity index / acid test IL*: excludes inventory, not always liquid
  without harming operations. Course thresholds: < 0.5 critical, 0.5–1 to
  monitor, > 1 optimal (indicative, not laws). Current ratio = AC/Pcor.

* Worked example
*ALFA*, reclassified balance sheet 2012 (EUR): Li 78,228; Ld 942,605;
inventory 1,228,990; AC 2,249,823; fixed assets 232,458; Pcor 1,363,449;
Pcon 472,746; equity 646,086; total 2,482,281.
| Indicator | Computation                       | Value    | Reading      |
|-----------+-----------------------------------+----------+--------------|
| MT        | 78,228 + 942,605 − 1,363,449      | −342,616 | cannot cover |
|           |                                   |          | short debts  |
| CCN       | 2,249,823 − 1,363,449             | 886,374  | positive     |
| IL        | (2,249,823 − 1,228,990)/1,363,449 | 0.75     | to monitor   |
Question: would you grant ALFA a 120-day loan in 2012? MT < 0 and IS = 2.84
(see [[id:financial-structure-ratios][structure ratios]]) look critical, but the rise of debt is mainly bank
debt, receivables from customers grew strongly 2011–2012 and tangible assets
grew 2012–2013: a firm trying to grow that banks were willing to finance — a
possible yes, to be checked in the notes. (2013 closed with a loss of 6,375.)

Simple case: cash 20, receivables 50, inventory 40, Pcor 80 → CCN 30, MT −10,
current ratio 1.375, acid test 0.875.

* Pitfall
Positive CCN can coexist with negative MT (inventory-heavy firms). Ratios are
snapshots at year end: seasonality and overdue receivables can hide.

* Recall
Draw the reclassified balance sheet; write MT, CCN, IL; compute them for ALFA
and argue the loan decision.
