#+title: The profit impact of purchasing savings
#+summary: Because material cost reductions raise the margin and cut inventory at the same time, a small purchasing saving can raise ROA more than a large sales increase; 1/ROS measures the leverage.

* Definition
\[ \text{ROA} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{operating margin}}{\text{sales}}}_{\text{ROS}}
   \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{sales}}{\text{total assets}}}_{\text{asset turnover}} \]
*Purchasing leverage effect*: the higher purchases are relative to revenue, the
bigger the impact of a purchase-cost reduction on profitability. Indicator:
\[ \text{leverage} = \frac{1}{\text{ROS}} \]
= euros of extra sales needed to match the margin from one euro saved on
materials. The lower the ROS, the stronger the effect (retail: low ROS
compensated by volumes, so purchasing is crucial). Incidence of purchases:
high (retail, metallurgy) over 60%; low (services) 10–20%.

* Worked example
Base data (EUR thousand):
| Income statement   | Base  | Sales +10% | Materials −5% |
|--------------------+-------+------------+---------------|
| Sales              | 6,050 | 6,655      | 6,050         |
| Direct labour      | 1,100 | 1,210      | 1,100         |
| Materials          | 3,300 | 3,630      | 3,135         |
| Overheads          | 1,000 | 1,100      | 1,000         |
| Cost of goods sold | 5,400 | 5,940      | 5,235         |
| Operating margin   | 650   | 715        | 815           |
| Inventory          | 1,100 | 1,100      | 1,045         |
| Receivables + cash | 750   | 750        | 750           |
| Fixed assets       | 2,800 | 2,800      | 2,800         |
| Total assets       | 4,650 | 4,650      | 4,595         |

| Ratio          | Base   | Sales +10% | Materials −5% |
|----------------+--------+------------+---------------|
| ROS            | 10.74% | 10.74%     | 13.47%        |
| Asset turnover | 1.301  | 1.431      | 1.317         |
| ROA            | 13.98% | 15.38%     | 17.74%        |
| ROA change     |        | +1.4 pts   | +3.8 pts      |
| Relative       |        | +10%       | +27%          |
Materials −5% saves 165 on cost (margin +25%) *and* values inventory 5% lower
(1,100 → 1,045), so both ROS and turnover improve. With
\( 1/\text{ROS} = 1/0.107 \approx 9 \): one euro saved on materials is worth about
nine euros of extra sales.
(The course's version reports 15.60% and 17.5% because it divides the +10% case
by 4,595 assets and rounds; the ranking and the conclusion are unchanged.)

* Model
Purchasing is strategic beyond cost: it affects product quality; customer
service depends on suppliers' reliability and lead times; power and
collaboration relations are involved; suppliers' technical skills help develop
products meeting the specifications. See [[id:profitability-ratios][ROI and ROA]].

* Pitfall
A sales increase raises margin *and* costs proportionally (ROS unchanged); a
purchasing saving raises ROS directly. The ratio 1/ROS is an approximation that
ignores fixed costs and capacity limits.
