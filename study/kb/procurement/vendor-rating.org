#+title: Vendor grids and weighted supplier ratings
#+summary: A vendor grid scores each supplier on weighted product and supplier criteria; the weighted sum ranks offers, as in Enel's vendor-rating index.

* Definition
\[ F_k = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \, c_{i,k} \qquad \sum_i w_i = 1 \]
\( F_k \) total score of supplier \( k \); \( w_i \) weight of criterion \( i \);
\( c_{i,k} \) score of supplier \( k \) on criterion \( i \) (5 excellent, 4 good,
3 adequate, 2 poor, 1 very poor); \( n \) criteria. Goals of supplier
assessment: select the best supplier, discover new suppliers (vendor list),
judge medium/long-term relations, stimulate improvement or a "new start".

* Worked example
*Example 1*
| Criterion            | Weight | Supplier 1 | Supplier 2 | Supplier 3 |
|----------------------+--------+------------+------------+------------|
| Quality (product)    | 30%    | 4          | 5          | 2          |
| Price (product)      | 25%    | 3          | 3          | 5          |
| Warranty (product)   | 15%    | 4          | 2          | 3          |
| Technical competence | 10%    | 4          | 5          | 1          |
| Flexibility          | 10%    | 5          | 3          | 2          |
| Financial situation  | 8%     | 3          | 2          | 4          |
| Production capacity  | 2%     | 4          | 4          | 5          |
| *Total*              | 100%   | *3.77*     | 3.59       | 3.02       |
Supplier 1: \( 1.20+0.75+0.60+0.40+0.50+0.24+0.08 = 3.77 \) → chosen.
(Recomputing Supplier 3 gives 3.02; a printed 3.62 does not match its scores.)

*Example 2* (14 criteria)
| Criterion            | W  | A | B | C |
|----------------------+----+---+---+---|
| Price                | 25 | 5 | 3 | 2 |
| Quality              | 20 | 2 | 3 | 5 |
| Warranty             | 5  | 5 | 2 | 3 |
| On-time delivery     | 15 | 4 | 2 | 3 |
| Quantity accuracy    | 5  | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Technical skills     | 5  | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Financial stability  | 5  | 4 | 5 | 2 |
| Quality certificates | 5  | 5 | 2 | 1 |
| Flexibility          | 3  | 2 | 2 | 5 |
| Workforce situation  | 3  | 2 | 4 | 5 |
| Production capacity  | 3  | 3 | 5 | 3 |
| Management skills    | 2  | 5 | 4 | 3 |
| Sales skills         | 2  | 4 | 2 | 5 |
| After-sales service  | 2  | 1 | 2 | 5 |
Totals: A = *3.71*, B = 2.99, C = 3.35 → supplier A, thanks to price (25%)
despite poor quality.

*Enel* uses vendor rating to monitor suppliers and contractors: the IVR (Vendor
Rating Index) is a weighted average of three categories — *quality*,
*punctuality*, *correctness*.

* Model
Steps: choose criteria (product and supplier) → assign weights (management
judgement or AHP pairwise comparisons) → score each supplier → weighted sum →
rank → sensitivity check on weights. Complement with [[id:total-cost-of-ownership][TCO]] for cost.

* Pitfall
Weights must sum to 100%. A high total can hide a fatal score (quality 2):
set minimum thresholds. Scores are judgements: document how they were given.

* Recall
Write the formula; recompute Supplier 1 and Supplier A; explain why A wins in
example 2 even with the worst quality.
