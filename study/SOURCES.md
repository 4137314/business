## Material inventory and boundaries

All 21 PDFs supplied in `asset/` were inventoried. Numbered files are not assumed
to be consecutive topics: 6–9 are absent, while the three named marketing decks
supply introduction, segmentation and targeting/positioning. `10.pdf` covers the
marketing mix. `11.pdf` repeats its first twenty PDF pages; the map cites the first
copy. The two named March purchasing decks cover operational tools and sourcing.
`12`–`19` cover operations, quality, improvement, digitalization, accounting,
project management and supply chains as mapped above.

`5.pdf` is a largely image-based guest presentation about the buyer's changing
role. It is retained as contextual reading; no unverified company metrics or
image-only assertions are incorporated into the concept notes. Elsewhere, the
company examples, matrices and classroom exercises shown in the decks (including
image-only pages) were read and restated explicitly in the notes, with figures
redrawn as TikZ diagrams; photographs and logos are not reproduced.

The historical `../organizzazione-e-gestione-aziendale/` question list and
January 2025 exam recollection were consulted only to identify useful revision
styles (concept comparisons, five-forces analysis and volume-variety analysis).
They are student-authored historical material, not evidence of the current exam
format or official solutions. No historical-only syllabus is silently merged
into the 2026 core material.

Break-even analysis, the simple Kanban sizing exercise, ROE, AHP context,
traditional FMEA risk scoring, Little's law, cost slope for crashing, EAC and
the bullwhip numerical illustration are supporting explanations. Numerical
cases taken from the decks are recomputed independently; additional MCQs and
exercises are newly written practice, not copied assessment questions.

## Corrections and conventions

- **Recomputed deck examples:** vendor grid example 1 prints 3.62 for supplier 3,
  but its scores give 3.02 (ranking unchanged). In the aluminium-oxide TCO,
  supplier A's net cost 152,000 corresponds to a 5% discount, not the printed
  6% (with 6% its TCO is 188,409; C still wins). In the purchasing-leverage case
  the +10% sales scenario is recomputed on unchanged assets of 4,650 (ROA
  15.38%, +10% relative) and the −5% materials scenario gives ROA 17.74%
  (+27%); the deck's 15.60% and 17.5% come from mixing asset bases. The
  value-stream example's listed times sum to 54 minutes (efficiency ≈ 24%),
  not 56 (23%). The SP example's final 03.03 table shows the payable already
  removed (total 25,600).

- **Accruals/deferrals:** `16.pdf` p. 31 reverses the descriptions of *ratei* and
  *risconti*. Its rental examples on pp. 32–33 are consistent with accruals for
  current-period amounts settled later and deferrals for amounts already settled
  but attributable to later periods. The note follows [OIC 18, paragraphs 3–6 and
  12–16](https://www.fondazioneoic.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2016-12-OIC-18-Ratei-e-Risconti.pdf).
- **Statements and extraordinary items:** the introductory three-document
  presentation is not treated as a complete statement of current reporting
  requirements. The notes explain cash flows separately. `17.pdf` p. 6 includes
  an old extraordinary-results section; this is not reproduced as a current
  statutory template. [OIC 12](https://www.fondazioneoic.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2022-05-OIC-12-Composizione-e-schemi-del-bilancio1.pdf)
  explains its removal from the income-statement scheme. Exact statutory
  reporting obligations and exceptions are outside these introductory notes.
- **EBIT/EBT:** `17.pdf` p. 32 labels profit before tax, after financing effects,
  “EBIT”. The note distinguishes EBIT from EBT and specifies a simplified
  reclassification rather than copying that label.
- **ROI:** `17.pdf` p. 33 gives an assets-minus-all-liabilities expression, which
  equals equity and is not a general operating invested-capital definition.
  Page 34 also prints `4,500,000 - 1,200,000 + 300,000 = 3,000,000`, although the
  arithmetic gives 3,600,000. The examples here use explicitly defined capital
  bases and independently calculated values; the incompatible expressions are
  not silently reconciled into one alleged course formula.
- **Ratio labels:** the material uses production value for ROS on p. 36 and
  all non-equity liabilities for ROD on p. 37. The note identifies those
  conventions alongside net-sales ROS and the distinction between total
  liabilities and interest-bearing debt. The p. 25 “solidity/autonomy” ratio is
  liabilities/equity, so a higher value means greater external dependence.
  Numerical thresholds are not universal guarantees of safety or solvency.
- **Six Sigma:** the 3.4 DPMO convention assumes a 1.5-standard-deviation shift;
  it is not the centred normal probability beyond six standard deviations.
  See the [ASQ explanation](https://asqasktheexperts.org/2016/10/07/six-sigma-statistical-meaning/).
- **Model limits:** descriptions of motivation, sourcing power, portfolio matrices
  and process fit are management models, not deterministic laws. Examples label
  assumptions and distinguish a useful framework from a proof of causation.

External checks above were performed on 2026-11-18. English wording and examples
are original summaries; original PDFs stay local and excluded from version control.
